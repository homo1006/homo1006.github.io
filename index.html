Guide · Power banks

Power banks that actually charge your laptop

Most people buy on the mAh figure. The number that decides whether a power bank can charge a laptop is its output in watts.

Ad · Contains affiliate links. As an Amazon Associate we earn from qualifying purchases.

What to check

  • Output in watts (W). Phones fast-charge at 20–30W. Most thin laptops need 45–65W over USB-C Power Delivery (PD).
  • Watt-hours (Wh), not just mAh. Wh is the real energy figure. Airlines usually allow power banks up to 100Wh in hand luggage without approval, and never in the hold.
  • USB-C in and out. Recharging the bank itself at 30W+ saves hours.
  • Ports you will use. Two USB-C ports lets you charge a laptop and phone together.

Guide · Smart home

Smart plugs that show what your appliances cost

A smart plug with energy monitoring tells you exactly how many kWh your tumble dryer, gaming PC or dehumidifier uses. That is where the savings start.

Ad · Contains affiliate links. As an Amazon Associate we earn from qualifying purchases.

What to check

  • Energy monitoring. Not every smart plug has it. The listing should mention kWh or power usage tracking.
  • UK Type G, rated 13A. Needed for heaters, kettles and dryers. Avoid plugs that only cover the next socket over.
  • Matter support. Works with Alexa, Google Home and Apple Home, so you are not locked into one app.
  • No hub needed. Wi-Fi plugs connect straight to your router.
