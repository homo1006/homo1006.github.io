Guide · Power banks

Power banks that actually charge your laptop

Most people buy on the mAh figure. The number that decides whether a power bank can charge a laptop is its output in watts.

Ad · Contains affiliate links. As an Amazon Associate we earn from qualifying purchases.

What to check

  • Output in watts (W). Phones fast-charge at 20–30W. Most thin laptops need 45–65W over USB-C Power Delivery (PD).
  • Watt-hours (Wh), not just mAh. Wh is the real energy figure. Airlines usually allow power banks up to 100Wh in hand luggage without approval, and never in the hold.
  • USB-C in and out. Recharging the bank itself at 30W+ saves hours.
  • Ports you will use. Two USB-C ports lets you charge a laptop and phone together.

Guide · Smart home

Smart plugs that show what your appliances cost

A smart plug with energy monitoring tells you exactly how many kWh your tumble dryer, gaming PC or dehumidifier uses. That is where the savings start.

Ad · Contains affiliate links. As an Amazon Associate we earn from qualifying purchases.

What to check

  • Energy monitoring. Not every smart plug has it. The listing should mention kWh or power usage tracking.
  • UK Type G, rated 13A. Needed for heaters, kettles and dryers. Avoid plugs that only cover the next socket over.
  • Matter support. Works with Alexa, Google Home and Apple Home, so you are not locked into one app.
  • No hub needed. Wi-Fi plugs connect straight to your router.

Guide · Winter at home

Stay warm and dry this winter for less

The energy price cap went up 4% on 1 October 2026. Heating the person, drying the air and heating only the rooms you use are three cheap ways to fight back.

Ad · Contains affiliate links. As an Amazon Associate we earn from qualifying purchases. We have not tested these products ourselves; running costs below are Which?'s published estimates.

What to check

  • Heated throws and electric blankets: timer and heat settings. A timer stops it running all night. Which? estimates heated throws cost about 2p–3p an hour to run, against 8p–76p for an electric heater.
  • Overnight use. Unplug an electric blanket before you sleep unless it has a thermostat designed for safe overnight use. Replace any blanket that shows wear or visible wires.
  • Dehumidifiers: litres per day, not tank size. Capacity is how much water it pulls from the air in a day. 7–12L suits a small flat with mild damp; 14–16L a bigger household; 18L+ if you dry laundry indoors.
  • Compressor or desiccant. Compressor (refrigerant) models are the UK norm for heated rooms. Desiccant models cope better in cold spaces such as garages and conservatories.
  • Humidistat and continuous drain. A humidistat holds the level you set, so it is not running for nothing. A drain hose saves emptying the tank.
  • Smart radiator valves: check what you already have. They swap onto existing thermostatic valves (TRVs); plain manual valves usually need a plumber. Most need the same brand's bridge, and a matching smart thermostat to switch the boiler.

Guide · Kitchen and energy

Cook for less with an air fryer or slow cooker

An electric oven heats a big empty box to cook a small meal. In Which?'s October 2026 tests, an air fryer did frozen chips for 8p against 23p in the oven. Here's how to pick the right size and skip the features you won't use.

Ad · Contains affiliate links. As an Amazon Associate we earn from qualifying purchases. We have not tested these products ourselves; running costs below are Which?'s published estimates at the 26.32p/kWh cap rate, and prices may have changed since, so check the listing.

What to check

  • Air fryer: basket size for your household. Which? tested models holding from 0.3kg to 2.4kg, and capped its chips test at 500g, enough for two to three portions. Cooking for four, or two dishes at once? Look at a dual-zone model.
  • Air fryer: how much you will really save. Which? measured roast chicken at 14p against 30p in the oven, and a jacket potato at 10p against 29p. A weekly roast in the air fryer saves about £8 a year, so a cheaper model pays for itself sooner. Tested models cost £30–£285.
  • Slow cooker: one litre per serving. That is Which?'s rule of thumb, so a 3.5L pot suits two or three and 6L+ suits a family or batch cooking.
  • Slow cooker: running costs vary more than you'd think. Which? puts an eight-hour stew at about 16p in an efficient model and about 40p in the least efficient, against roughly 30p an hour for an oven.
  • Slow cooker features worth having. A keep-warm setting stops food overcooking if you're late home. A removable pot is easier to wash, and a sear function lets you brown meat in the same pot.

What to check

  • Is the deal real? Which? tracked 175 home, tech and health appliances around the 2024 sales and found 83% were the same price or cheaper at least once outside the four-week Black Friday period. Compare the price with the last six months, not with the "was" figure.
  • Which phone do they have? AirTags set up on an iPhone or iPad. Google's Find Hub tags are for Android. Check before you wrap it.
  • Charger watts per port. The 100W on the box is usually shared between the ports. Most thin laptops need 45–65W, so check what one port gives with everything plugged in, and whether they still need a USB-A port.
  • Can it come back? Online orders of non-faulty goods can normally be returned within 14 days of delivery, which may run out before Christmas if you buy now. Which? also warns that retailers can restrict returns on headphones for hygiene reasons, so check the policy first.
